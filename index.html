Pro Equipment

The Machines Behind a Real Dry-Out

Household fans move air. Restoration equipment moves moisture — out of walls, floors, and the air itself — and proves the result with readings. This is what rolls off our trucks on every Meridian job:

Truck-Mounted Extraction

High-lift vacuum pulls water out of carpet and padding in a single pass — hundreds of gallons gone before the first fan is even placed.

Air Movers & Axial Fans

Directed high-velocity airflow across wet surfaces speeds evaporation and pushes moisture toward the dehumidifiers instead of deeper into the structure.

LGR Dehumidifiers

Commercial low-grain refrigerant units pull moisture out of the air around the clock, so framing, drywall, and subfloor — not just surfaces — reach dry standard.

Moisture Meters & Thermal Imaging

Pin and pinless meters plus infrared cameras find hidden water behind walls and under floors — and verify dry before anything gets closed up.

Local Knowledge

Why Water Damage Is So Common in Meridian

Meridian (population 142,988, Census July 2025) has the newest housing in America — median build year 2007, 40% of homes built in 2010 or later, only 7% built before 1980, median home value $531,600. That changes the water story completely: Meridian's damage comes from new-build failure modes, not old-pipe decay.

New builds failing together

Meridian was farm ground into the late 1990s. Most of what floods now is post-2000 construction: PEX, plastic-bodied angle stops, braided appliance connectors — and whole subdivisions whose water heaters reach end of life within a few years of each other.

Irrigation canals & a high water table

The Nampa & Meridian Irrigation District runs 500+ miles of canals serving 523 subdivisions, and most homes have pressurized irrigation. A century of irrigation seepage keeps the water table high — leaks, seepage, and damp crawl spaces follow.

Freeze–thaw winters

Boise's record low is −25°F (December 22, 1990), and Treasure Valley winters hold at or below about 20°F for hours at a time. Daytime highs above freezing, overnight lows in the teens: pipes in crawl spaces, attics, and unheated garages split when the ice thaws.

Spring snowmelt into crawl spaces

Meridian homes are overwhelmingly crawl-space and slab builds, not basements. Spring snowmelt and sump-pump failure during runoff push water under the house first — where it rots framing and grows mold out of sight.
